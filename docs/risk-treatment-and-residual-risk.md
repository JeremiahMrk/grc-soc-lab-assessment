# Risk Treatment and Residual Risk

## Treatment Planning

The treatment phase uses the risk register and prioritization results to decide what should be done about identified risks.

Supported treatment options include:

- Mitigate
- Accept
- Transfer
- Avoid

During the documented treatment phase, **12 selected risks** received active treatment planning. Lower-priority risks could be accepted and monitored when justified, while selected medium risks were still treated when they affected administrative integrity or monitoring reliability.

## Treatment-Plan Structure

Private treatment records document items such as:

- risk ID and owner
- treatment decision
- planned control or corrective action
- target / priority
- implementation evidence
- expected or actual residual-risk reasoning

A treatment decision is not the same as completed remediation.

## Evidence Before Risk Reduction

The post-treatment model follows four important rules:

1. **Keep the same scoring scale.** Likelihood and impact remain on the original 1–5 scale so before/after comparisons remain meaningful.
2. **No verified evidence means no risk reduction.** If there is no post-treatment evidence demonstrating that the control operates, residual risk remains at the inherent level.
3. **Impact is not reduced casually.** Most controls primarily reduce likelihood; reducing impact requires a defensible reason and evidence.
4. **Maintain comparable populations.** Before/after reporting must distinguish a like-for-like comparison from changes caused by newly discovered risks.

## Residual Risk

The final residual-risk work updates the 24-risk register after treatment and verification.

The purpose is not to claim that all risks were eliminated. Instead, it records which risks changed, which did not, and why.

The public repository intentionally avoids publishing sensitive risk statements, credential examples, exact administrative paths, or raw remediation commands.

## Key Portfolio Takeaway

This work demonstrates the difference between:

**finding a problem → assigning risk → deciding treatment → implementing a control → validating evidence → reassessing residual risk**

That chain is the core GRC value of the project.
